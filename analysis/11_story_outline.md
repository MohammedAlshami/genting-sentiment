# The story, in website sections

**Core correction:** the data does **not** support “the mood broke first.” Sentiment stayed positive for most of the decline and only eroded after the cash call. The story is stronger and more honest as: **the crowd stayed positive too long.**

## 01 — The crowd followed the price.

**Visual:** real monthly sentiment line (range roughly +0.05 to +0.19) against GENM price on the same screen. The price falls; sentiment stays high, then sags.

**Copy:**
> Retail did not call the top. It stayed positive through the first year of the decline.
>
> Monthly sentiment peaked around July 2025 at +0.19 — while the stock was already drifting. It was still +0.13 in December 2025, after the privatisation bid failed. Only in late 2026 did the bearish share climb from ~16% to ~27.5%.
>
> Sentiment moved **with** the price (same-month correlation 0.50 for GENTING, 0.62 for GENM), not ahead of it. The crowd was a follower, not a warning system.

This is the intellectual hook of the whole page: **retail sentiment is a coincident indicator, not a leading one.**

# What not to say

- Don’t say sentiment **predicted** the fall. It didn’t.
- Don’t call this a **poll**. It’s a corpus of public posts, weighted by platform, not a representative sample.
- Don’t say “the mood broke first.” The price broke first; the mood followed.
- Don’t present the keyword polarity as a trained model. The real score is the VADER/lexicon pass, and it still has limits.

# What makes the page different

The insight is not “Genting fell.” Everyone knows that.

The insight is: **75,000 people watched it fall, stayed bullish for a year, and only capitulated when the company asked them for money.** That is a much better story — and it is what the data actually says.
